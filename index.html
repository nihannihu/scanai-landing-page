The Market & Engineering Bottleneck

Why 3D C-Arm & Spine Navigation Projects Fail in Practice

Motorized C-arms capture dozens of orbital shots — yet most OEMs stall for years trying to produce sharp Axial 3D spine CT. Scan AI turns ordinary motorized C-arms into navigation-grade 3D scanners purely in software.

01

C-Arm Gravity Sag (±6 to ±8.5 mm)

Open C-arms flex under gravity during rotation. Standard circular math smears bone walls into blurry double edges, making pedicle checks unsafe.

02

Non-360° Short-Scan & Truncation

Operating tables block full 360° rotation (~198° spins) and patient hips exceed detector width — creating severe cupping and shading.

03

Titanium Implant Starbursts

Metal pedicle screws overload filters and project dark streaks directly over the spinal canal right when surgeons check nerve safety.

04

Optical Camera Line-of-Sight Failure

Legacy ₹2–4 Crore navigation towers rely on infrared spheres that break line-of-sight when OT staff stand around the surgical field.

Robotic Spine Surgery Vision — OmniSpine™ Robotics

Unified 9-DOF C-Arm Pose + 6-DOF Robotic Spine Guidance

Locking patient CT/CBCT, C-arm orbital geometry, and a tremor-free 6-DOF bedside arm into one closed-loop coordinate frame.

What 9-DOF + 6-DOF Unification Achieves in Surgery

  • 9-DOF Projective Calibration: Captures exact 3D source position, detector tilt & focal flex (0.12 px) so C-arm and patient spine stay mathematically co-registered.
  • 6-DOF Robotic Arm Alignment: Positions a rigid cannulated guide tube across all 3 translations + 3 rotations directly over the planned pedicle axis.
  • Eliminates Human Tremor & Skiving: Locks trajectory angle firmly on sloped cortical bone, reducing screw breach risk compared to freehand placement.
  • Zero Optical Camera Clutter: Optical-free XVR/9-DOF registration works seamlessly around crowded surgical teams without sphere occlusion.
